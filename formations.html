Team structure

Football Formations

Formation numbers generally show defenders, midfielders and forwards, not the goalkeeper.

4-3-3

Four defenders, three midfielders and three forwards. It can provide width, pressing options and attacking support.

Possible weakness: midfielders may need to cover large spaces.

4-2-3-1

Four defenders, two deeper midfielders, three attacking midfielders and one striker. It can offer a balanced structure.

4-4-2

Two banks of four can make the team compact and organised, with two forwards working together.

4-1-2-1-2 Narrow

A defensive midfielder, two central midfielders, an attacking midfielder and two strikers. It can dominate central spaces but may leave the flanks open unless full-backs provide width.

3-5-2

Three centre-backs, wing-backs, central midfielders and two forwards. Wing-backs often provide the team's width.

5-3-2

Often used for a deeper defensive shape. The team needs a plan to move forward after regaining possession.

Remember: no formation is automatically the best. Choose based on your players, strengths, opponent and coach's instructions.
